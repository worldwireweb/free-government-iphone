Understanding the Lifeline Program

What the Lifeline Program Is

The Lifeline program is a federal initiate created by the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) in 1985. Originally designed for landlines, it has evolved to support wireless services. Its primary goal is to make communication services affordable for low-income consumers, ensuring they have the security and opportunities that connectivity brings—jobs, healthcare, and family.

How Lifeline Helps Provide Free Phones and Service

Lifeline provides a monthly discount of up to $9.25 (or up to $34.25 on Tribal lands) on phone or internet service. Providers often combine this subsidy with their own business models to offer plans that include not just the discount, but a completely free monthly plan with talk, text, and data, plus a free device like the iPhone 15 Pro Max as a signup bonus.

Key Government Agencies Involved

Federal Communications Commission (FCC): The FCC sets the rules and policy for the program.

Universal Service Administrative Company (USAC): An independent organization designated by the FCC to administer the Universal Service Fund and the Lifeline program, including managing the National Verifier system.

Eligibility Criteria

Income-Based Eligibility

You can qualify for a free government phone if your household's total annual income is at or below 135% of the Federal Poverty Guidelines. The specific dollar amount depends on your household size and the state you live in.

Program-Based Eligibility

Participation in specific government assistance programs is the most common way to qualify. If you use:

  • SNAP (Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program) / Food Stamps
  • Medicaid
  • SSI (Supplemental Security Income)
  • Federal Public Housing Assistance (FPHA)
  • Veterans Pension and Survivors Benefit

You are automatically eligible.

Documentation Needed to Qualify

To prove your eligibility, you will need to submit copies of official documents. These might include a benefit award letter, a tax return, or pay stubs.
